Oral health awareness

Prevention is the best treatment

Most dental disease is preventable. Understand the daily routine, what happens when we neglect it, and how modern dentistry rescues complex cases.

Your daily routine

🌅
Morning
Brush for 2 minutes with fluoride toothpaste, clean your tongue, and drink water.
🍽️
After meals
Rinse with water or chew sugar-free gum. After acidic food or drinks, wait about 30 minutes before brushing.
🌙
Night
Floss or use interdental brushes, brush for 2 minutes, then skip snacks and drinks other than water.
🗓️
Regularly
Replace your toothbrush (or brush head) every ~3 months and visit your dentist for checkups and cleaning.
Comprehensive oral hygiene
  • Plaque is a sticky bacterial film; it hardens into tartar that only a dental cleaning can remove.
  • Use a soft brush and light pressure; hard scrubbing wears enamel and damages gums.
  • Brushing reaches about 60% of tooth surfaces — cleaning between teeth covers the rest.
  • Limit sugary snacks and sipping sweet or acidic drinks throughout the day.
Medical risks of neglect
  • Cavities that reach the nerve cause severe pain, abscesses and infection.
  • Gum disease (gingivitis → periodontitis) destroys the bone that holds teeth and is a leading cause of tooth loss.
  • Chronic gum inflammation is associated with diabetes complications and cardiovascular disease.
  • Persistent bad breath, bleeding gums or loose teeth are warning signs — don't wait for pain.
Daily prevention strategies
  • Fluoride toothpaste, professional fluoride varnish and dental sealants for children and high-risk teeth.
  • Wear a mouthguard for sports and a night guard if you grind your teeth.
  • Avoid smoking and vaping; they raise the risk of gum disease and oral cancer.
Advanced treatments for complex cases

Root canal treatment: the dentist removes infected or inflamed pulp, cleans and disinfects the canals, seals them, and usually protects the tooth with a crown. It saves the natural tooth and relieves pain.

Dental implants: a titanium post is placed in the jawbone and fuses with it over several months (osseointegration); an abutment and a custom crown then replace the missing tooth. Healthy gums and enough bone are required.

Brushing with a manual toothbrush

  1. Place the bristles at a 45° angle to the gumline with a pea-sized amount of fluoride toothpaste.
  2. Use gentle short strokes, about the width of one tooth, on the outer surfaces.
  3. Repeat on the inner and chewing surfaces; tilt the brush vertically for the front teeth.
  4. Brush for 2 minutes in total, then spit — don't rinse with water.

Tip: a soft brush protects enamel and gums. Replace it every ~3 months.

Using an electric toothbrush

  1. Wet the head, add toothpaste, then place it on the gumline before switching on.
  2. Glide slowly from tooth to tooth, about 2–3 seconds each — don't scrub.
  3. Let the brush do the work with very light pressure; follow the pressure sensor if you have one.
  4. Spend about 30 seconds on each quadrant (2 minutes total), including the tongue side.

Tip: sonic or oscillating heads clean well, but technique still matters.

Flossing step by step

  1. Take about 45 cm of floss; wind most around one middle finger and the rest around the other.
  2. Guide it gently between teeth with a back-and-forth motion — never snap it into the gum.
  3. Curve it into a C-shape against one tooth and slide up and down below the gumline.
  4. Repeat on the neighbouring tooth, then use a fresh section of floss for the next gap.

Tip: slight bleeding in the first days is common with inflamed gums and usually improves; see your dentist if it persists.

Using a water flosser

  1. Fill the reservoir with warm water, choose a low pressure and attach the tip.
  2. Lean over the sink, lips slightly closed so water can flow out of your mouth, then switch on.
  3. Aim the tip at the gumline at about 90° and pause briefly between each pair of teeth.
  4. Follow the gumline across the outer then inner surfaces of the upper and lower arches.

Tip: water flossers are a great aid for braces and implants, but they complement — not replace — brushing.

Rinsing with mouthwash

  1. Brush and floss first; use mouthwash at a different time from toothpaste if it is fluoride-based.
  2. Pour the amount on the label (usually 15–20 ml) into the cap or a cup.
  3. Swish and gargle for about 30 seconds so it reaches every area.
  4. Spit it out — never swallow — and avoid eating or drinking for about 30 minutes.

Tip: mouthwash is an extra step, not a substitute for brushing. Alcohol-free options are gentler on dry mouths.

Interdental brush for braces

  1. Pick a size that slides in snugly without force; your dentist can advise the right one.
  2. Insert the brush gently under the archwire, between the bracket and the gum.
  3. Move it in and out 2–3 times, then sweep around each bracket from above and below.
  4. Rinse the brush and repeat for every tooth, then finish with your normal brushing.

Tip: trapped plaque around brackets can leave white marks on enamel — clean carefully every day.

Orthodontics hub

Straighter teeth, healthier bite

Orthodontic treatment gently moves teeth into better positions. Explore the two most popular options.

Traditional metal braces

Brackets & archwire

Small metal brackets are bonded to each tooth. A thin archwire runs through them and applies gentle, continuous pressure; the dentist changes wires and ties over time to guide teeth into place.

Separators (spacers)

Tiny rubber rings or springs placed between back teeth about 1–2 weeks before bands are fitted. They create just enough space for the bands. Mild soreness is normal; avoid sticky food and do not remove them.

Elastics (rubber bands)

Elastics link the upper and lower jaws to correct the bite. They must be worn as instructed and changed daily. Different patterns do different jobs:

Class II

From the upper front to the lower back; helps correct an overbite.

Class III

From the lower front to the upper back; helps correct an underbite.

Vertical

Connects upper and lower teeth to close an open bite.

Cross

Pulls across the arch to fix a crossbite and centre the midline.

Benefits of metal braces: highly effective for simple to complex cases, fixed in place so no reliance on remembering to wear them, durable, and usually the most affordable option.

Invisalign (clear aligners)

How it works

A series of custom, transparent, removable aligners. Each set is slightly different from the last and moves teeth in small steps. You switch to the next aligner every 1–2 weeks.

Attachments

Small tooth-coloured bumps bonded to certain teeth give the aligner something to grip, helping it rotate or lift teeth precisely.

Wear time

Wear them about 20–22 hours a day and take them out only to eat, drink anything other than water, and clean your teeth.
